\chapter{Invariant-Tree Algorithm}
\label{ch:hybridApproach}

The invariant-tree algorithm idea of \citet{MejstrikReif2025HybridApproachJSR} combines a polytope norm with the finite-tree certificate.
A separate test tree can be chosen for each vertex, with the product bound proved by induction.

\section{Overview}
\subsection{Combination Strategies}
\label{sec:hybridStrategies}

The invariant-polytope algorithm constructs a norm adapted to the matrix family, while the finite-tree algorithm controls products by leaf decompositions.
One can first construct a polytope and then search for a tree in its norm.
The concurrent approach of \citet{MejstrikReif2025HybridApproachJSR} instead tests one-step images with product trees while constructing the polytope.
The variant below retains every outside image and uses its tree test to decide whether further expansion is needed.

\subsection{Problem Formulation}
\label{sec:hybridProblemFormulation}

After the positive candidate scaling in Section~\ref{sec:introPreprocessing}, the normalized family has JSR at least $1$.
We omit the tildes in this chapter and write $\mathcal{A}$ for that normalized family.
The remaining task is to establish $\JSR(\mathcal{A})\leq1$.
We do so by bounding every length-$k$ product in one norm by a common polynomial $p(k)$.
No irreducibility assumption is required for this upper-bound certificate.

A fixed tree works in operator norm when all its uncovered leaves map every vertex into the polytope.
Each starting vertex can instead use its own tree.
To prove the resulting bound, we replace each completed leaf image by an absolutely convex combination of vertices and apply the appropriate tree to each shorter remaining product.

\section{Algorithmic Formulation}
\label{sec:hybridAlgorithm}

\begin{definition}
    \label{def:oneVBoundedTree}
    Let $v\in\R^d$ and fix a vector norm.
    A finite $\AG$-tree is \emph{$1$-$v$-bounded} if
    \[
        \max_{L\in\mathcal{L}(T)}\lVert Lv\rVert\leq1,
        \qquad \lVert Lv\rVert=\sup_{P\in L}\lVert Pv\rVert.
    \]
    It is \emph{strictly $1$-$v$-bounded} when this maximum is strictly less than $1$.
\end{definition}

The starting set $V$ is finite and spans $\R^d$, so $\mathrm{co}_{\mathrm{s}}(V)$ is a norm unit ball.
The set $Q$ contains the points whose one-step images still need to be processed.
For each $v\in Q$, we examine the finitely many images $w=A_iv$.
If an image lies outside the current polytope, we add it to $V$ and test a tree at $w$ in the enlarged polytope norm.
A successful test certifies $w$ without adding it to $Q$.
Otherwise $w$ enters $Q$ and its one-step images are processed later.

\begin{algorithm}[ht]
    \caption{Invariant-tree certificate search}
    \label{alg:hybridApproach}
    \begin{algorithmic}
        \State \Input{Normalized $\mathcal{A}$, admissible generators, finite spanning set $V$}
        \State $Q\gets V$
        \While{$Q\neq\emptyset$}
            \State Remove a vertex $v$ from $Q$
            \For{$i=1,\dots,n$}
                \State $w\gets A_iv$
                \If{$\lVert w\rVert_V>1$}
                    \State $V\xleftarrow{\cup}\{w\}$
                    \State Choose an $\AG$-test tree $T_w$
                    \If{the test proves $\lVert Lw\rVert_V\leq1$ for every $L\in\mathcal{L}(T_w)$}
                        \State Store $T_w$ as a certificate for $w$
                    \Else
                        \State $Q\xleftarrow{\cup}\{w\}$
                    \EndIf
                \EndIf
            \EndFor
            \State Store the one-level positive tree as a certificate for $v$
        \EndWhile
        \State \Return $V$ and its stored trees
    \end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

Each tree test must bound whole leaf families, including all represented generator powers.
Failure to establish the bound only queues $w$ for ordinary expansion and need not produce a violating leaf image.
After processing $v$, all its one-step images belong to the enlarged polytope.
The tree consisting of the root and all $n$ positive children is therefore $1$-$v$-bounded.
A previously stored certificate remains valid when the polytope grows.
Every point in $V$ is thus either awaiting processing or has a stored certificate.
If the search returns after finitely many steps, every final point has a valid tree in the final norm.

\begin{remark}[Retaining or omitting certified images]
    \label{rem:hybridVertexUpdates}
    Algorithm~\ref{alg:hybridApproach} keeps a new outside image $w$ in $V$ even when its tree test succeeds.
    It then omits $w$ only from $Q$.
    Enlarging the unit ball can only decrease the norm of any fixed vector, which may help later tree tests close.
    The additional points also increase storage and may make polytope-membership tests more expensive.

    Algorithm~3.1 of \citet{MejstrikReif2025HybridApproachJSR} tests $w=A_iv$ in the current polytope before inserting it.
    If its tree closes, $w$ is omitted from both $V$ and the pending set.
    This is justified by attaching its test tree below the $i$th positive child of the tree at $v$.
    The resulting leaf images have the form $Pw=PA_iv$ and already lie in the current polytope.
    This rule can store fewer points, but it forgoes the enlargement obtained by retaining $w$.
    Neither rule is known here to be more efficient in general.
    The experiments in Chapter~\ref{ch:numericalTesting} use the toolbox's existing update rule and do not compare these two variants.
\end{remark}

\begin{example}[Different trees and a branching leaf image]
    \label{ex:hybridVertexTrees}
    Use the matrices $A,B$ and generator $G=A$ from Example~\ref{ex:finiteJordanTree}, now with
    \[
        v_1=e_1,\qquad v_2=e_1+e_2,\qquad V=\{v_1,v_2\}.
    \]
    The vertices form a basis, and the polytope norm is
    \[
        \lVert(x,y)^\top\rVert_V=|x-y|+|y|.
    \]
    For $v_1$, choose the tree $T_{v_1}$ consisting of the root and the two positive leaves.
    Its leaf images satisfy $Av_1=v_1$ and $Bv_1=0$, so it is $1$-$v_1$-bounded.
    This tree fails at $v_2$, since $Av_2=v_1+v_2$ has norm $2$.
    For $v_2$, choose the generator tree $T_{v_2}$ of Example~\ref{ex:finiteJordanTree}.
    Its only uncovered leaf family gives
    \[
        BA^rv_2=Bv_2=-\tfrac12v_1+\tfrac12v_2,
        \qquad r\in\N_0.
    \]
    The absolute coefficient sum is $1$, so this tree is $1$-$v_2$-bounded.
    Both trees satisfy the non-strict bound.

    For the product $P=A^3BA^2BA^4$, the first leaf factor is $BA^4$.
    Writing $Q=A^3BA^2$ gives
    \[
        Pv_2=-\tfrac12Qv_1+\tfrac12Qv_2,
        \qquad |Q|=6<11=|P|,
    \]
    where the lengths refer to the displayed words.
    The two remaining images are treated using $T_{v_1}$ and $T_{v_2}$, respectively.
    At each further split the absolute coefficients again sum to at most $1$.
    When the remaining word is $A^q$, its images satisfy
    \[
        A^qv_1=v_1,\qquad A^qv_2=qv_1+v_2,
        \qquad \lVert A^q\rVert_V=q+1.
    \]
    The recursion stops at a word represented by a node, where the common polynomial bound applies.
\end{example}

\section{Termination Theory}
\label{sec:hybridTerminationResults}

The common-tree certificate is established by \citet[Theorem 2.7]{MejstrikReif2025HybridApproachJSR}.
Their Algorithm 3.1 allows the test tree to depend on the vertex.
Lemma~\ref{lem:finitePrefixLeaf} supplies the word decomposition needed to prove closure for different trees at different vertices.

\begin{theorem}[Vertex-wise tree certificate]
    \label{thm:hybridTermination}
    Let $V$ be a finite set spanning $\R^d$.
    For each $v\in V$, let $T_v$ be a finite $\AG$-tree with generators of spectral radius at most $1$.
    If every $T_v$ is $1$-$v$-bounded in the final polytope norm, then $\JSR(\mathcal{A})\leq1$.
    In particular, a finite successful return of Algorithm~\ref{alg:hybridApproach} gives this upper bound.
\end{theorem}

The theorem applies once the stated trees and vertex set have been found.
The polytope need not be invariant under each matrix in $\mathcal{A}$.

\subsection{Proof Strategy}
\label{subsec:hybridProofOutline}

There are finitely many vertices and finite trees, so the node bounds can be combined into one polynomial.
A word encoded by a node is controlled immediately.
Otherwise a nonempty leaf factor maps its starting vertex into the polytope.
Expressing that image as a combination of vertices leaves shorter words, to which induction applies.
The coefficient bound prevents the recursive splits from increasing the common estimate.

\subsection{Auxiliary Termination Results}

\begin{lemma}[Common bound at all vertices]
    \label{lem:hybridArbitraryProductBound}
    Under the hypotheses of Theorem~\ref{thm:hybridTermination}, there is a positive, monotonically nondecreasing polynomial $p$ such that
    \[
        \lVert A_Iv\rVert_V\leq p(k)
        \quad\text{for }v\in V,\ I\in\mathcal{I}^k,\ k\in\N_0.
    \]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
    Work throughout in the norm induced by $\mathrm{co}_{\mathrm{s}}(V)$.
    Apply Lemma~\ref{lem:finitePolynomialBound} to each $T_v$ and choose
    \[
        p(x)=1+\sum_{v\in V}p_{T_v}(x).
    \]
    This single polynomial bounds the operator norms of all products encoded by nodes of any selected tree.
    It also satisfies $p(0)\geq1$.

    We prove the vertex bound by induction on $k$.
    At $k=0$, it follows from $\lVert v\rVert_V\leq1$.
    Suppose it holds for every smaller length, and fix $I\in\mathcal{I}^k$ and $v\in V$.
    If the word $I$ is encoded by a node of $T_v$, then
    \[
        \lVert A_Iv\rVert_V\leq\lVert A_I\rVert_V\lVert v\rVert_V\leq p(k).
    \]
    Otherwise Lemma~\ref{lem:finitePrefixLeaf} gives $I=[U,J]$, where $U$ is a nonempty uncovered-leaf word of $T_v$ and $|J|<k$.
    The leaf condition places $A_Uv$ in $\mathrm{co}_{\mathrm{s}}(V)$.
    Hence real coefficients $c_w$ exist with
    \[
        A_Uv=\sum_{w\in V}c_ww,\qquad\sum_{w\in V}|c_w|\leq1.
    \]
    Applying the induction hypothesis at every vertex $w$ and using monotonicity gives
    \[
        \lVert A_Iv\rVert_V
        \leq\sum_{w\in V}|c_w|\,\lVert A_Jw\rVert_V
        \leq p(|J|)\sum_{w\in V}|c_w|
        \leq p(k).
    \]
    This completes the induction.
\end{proof}

Each recursive branch ends with one remainder bounded by $p(k)$.
The absolute coefficient sums are at most $1$, so combining the branches preserves this bound, as in Example~\ref{ex:hybridVertexTrees}.

\subsection{Main Termination Result}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:hybridTermination}]
    Since $V$ is finite and spans $\R^d$, its symmetrized convex hull is the unit ball of a norm.
    Every $x$ in this ball can be written as $x=\sum_{v\in V}c_vv$ with $\sum_{v\in V}|c_v|\leq1$.
    Lemma~\ref{lem:hybridArbitraryProductBound} gives
    \[
        \lVert A_Ix\rVert_V
        \leq\sum_{v\in V}|c_v|\,\lVert A_Iv\rVert_V
        \leq p(k),\qquad I\in\mathcal{I}^k.
    \]
    Taking the supremum over the unit ball yields $\lVert A_I\rVert_V\leq p(k)$ for every word of length $k$.
    Consequently,
    \[
        \JSR(\mathcal{A})\leq\lim_{k\to\infty}p(k)^{1/k}=1.
    \]
    In Algorithm~\ref{alg:hybridApproach}, each new point is either certified by its test tree or queued for expansion.
    Processing a queued point certifies it with the one-level positive tree.
    All stored certificates remain valid as the polytope grows, so an empty queue supplies the hypotheses of the theorem.
\end{proof}

For a normalized candidate with averaged spectral radius $1$, the three-member inequality supplies the matching lower bound.
The JSR is then exactly $1$, and homogeneity returns the value for the original family.

\begin{corollary}
    If every selected tree consists of the root and the $n$ one-letter positive leaves, its vertex-wise closure condition is precisely invariant-polytope closure.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
    In this case the conditions are $A_iv\in\mathrm{co}_{\mathrm{s}}(V)$ for all $i$ and $v\in V$.
    By linearity and the absolute coefficient bound, these are equivalent to invariance of the entire polytope under every $A_i$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}
    If all selected trees coincide, the certificate also follows from a single prefix--leaf decomposition in operator norm.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
    Every leaf matrix $P$ then satisfies $Pv\in\mathrm{co}_{\mathrm{s}}(V)$ for every $v\in V$.
    The same convex-combination argument gives $\lVert P\rVert_V\leq1$.
    The common tree is therefore $1$-bounded in operator norm, and Theorem~\ref{thm:finiteTreeTermination} applies.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Relation between the three methods]
    If every test tree consists of the root and its $n$ positive children as leaves, the hybrid algorithm recovers ordinary IPA.
    Every outside image is added to $V$.
    Its tree test succeeds exactly when all its one-step images already lie in the polytope, so omitting it from $Q$ only skips an expansion that would add no points.

    If the same tree is used at every vertex, the closure condition is that of the finite-tree algorithm with $\lVert\cdot\rVert_V$ as the chosen matrix norm.
    Here $\lVert\cdot\rVert_V$ is the operator norm induced by the polytope $\mathrm{co}_{\mathrm{s}}(V)$.
    For a fixed $V$, searching for such a common tree is therefore a finite-tree search in that norm.
\end{remark}

\section{Implementation Considerations}
\label{sec:hybridImplementation}

\begin{definition}
    \label{def:hybridMinimalTree}
    For one fixed generator word $H$, a \emph{minimal generator tree} consists of the root, its generator child, the positive path spelling $H$, and every positive sibling required along that path.
    The completed path is covered by the generator node.
    The notation is $T_{\AG}$, shortened to $T_{\mathcal{G}}$ when the base family and generator word are understood.
\end{definition}

The toolbox constructs a minimal tree by default and also accepts manually supplied leaf words.
For the main numerical example, two generator trees are supplied, and a vertex may close under either one.
Section~\ref{sec:numericsPrimaryTree} verifies their tree structure and covered leaves.
The toolbox does not validate user-supplied words automatically.

The tested revision requires diagonalizable generators with periodic leading eigenvalues.
It splits a generator-power image into a periodic leading part and a decaying remainder.
The stable eigenvalues and norm equivalence bound the remainder, leaving finitely many initial powers to test.
This is a restricted implementation of the verification strategy in \citet[Section 3.3]{MejstrikReif2025HybridApproachJSR}.
The tested implementation therefore excludes the defective generator in Example~\ref{ex:finiteJordanTree}.

Iteration and resource limits can stop a run without a closed certificate.
It reports numerical lower and upper bounds when available.
An external timeout may prevent any return.
Such outcomes do not establish mathematical nontermination.
Candidate search and rejection remain part of the implementation, as in ordinary IPA.

The cost depends on generator spectra, leaf words, and polytope-norm solves.
The polynomial bound in the proof concerns matrix-product growth, not the worst-case running time of the algorithm.
Chapter~\ref{ch:numericalTesting} measures the cost for the archived test families.
