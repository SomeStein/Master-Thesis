\chapter{Finite-Tree Algorithm}
\label{ch:finiteTree}

The finite-tree method of \citet{MoellerReif2014TreeBasedJSR} represents families of matrix products by nodes of a finite tree.
Its product decomposition is also used in Chapter~\ref{ch:hybridApproach}.

\section{Overview and Tree Notation}
\label{sec:finiteNotationDefinitions}

The aim is to prove $\JSR(\mathcal{A})\leq1$.
A long product is split into completed leaf factors of norm at most $1$ and one remaining factor controlled polynomially in its length.
Irreducibility is not required for this certificate.

\begin{definition}
    \label{def:treeGraph}
    A \emph{rooted tree} is a connected, acyclic graph with a distinguished node called the \emph{root}.
    The preceding node on the unique path from the root to a nonroot node is its \emph{parent}.
    The following nodes are its \emph{children}.
    A node without children is a \emph{leaf}.
    Every other node is \emph{internal}.
\end{definition}

The full product tree $T_{\mathcal{A}}$ has one node for each positive index word $I\in\bigcup_{k\geq0}\mathcal{I}^k$.
Its root is the empty word $[]$, with $A_{[]}=I_d$.
A child appends one index, so the corresponding matrix is multiplied on the left.
Different words remain different nodes even when their matrices coincide.

\subsection{Generators and Encoded Words}

We fix a finite generator set $\mathcal{G}=\{G_1,\dots,G_m\}$.
Each generator comes with a fixed nonempty positive word $H_i\in\mathcal{I}^{k_i}$, where $k_i\geq1$, and satisfies
\[
    G_i=A_{H_i},\qquad \rho(G_i)\leq1,\qquad 1\leq i\leq m.
\]
This is equivalent to $\rho(G_i)^{1/k_i}\leq1$.

\begin{definition}
    \label{def:extendedMultiIndexSet}
    For negative indices we write $A_{-i}=G_i$.
    The extended index alphabet is $\mathcal{J}=\{-m,\dots,-1,1,\dots,n\}$.
    For $J=[j_1,\dots,j_l]\in\mathcal{J}^l$, let $\mathcal{W}(J)$ be the family of positive words obtained by replacing each positive entry $j_s$ by $[j_s]$ and each negative entry $-i$ by $H_i$ repeated $e_s\in\N_0$ times.
    Repetition zero contributes the empty word.
    The corresponding matrix set is
    \[
        A_J=\{A_I:I\in\mathcal{W}(J)\}
        =\{A_{j_l}^{e_l}\cdots A_{j_1}^{e_1}:
        e_s=1\text{ for }j_s>0,\ e_s\in\N_0\text{ for }j_s<0\}.
    \]
    For $J=[]$, these families are $\mathcal{W}([])=\{[]\}$ and $A_{[]}=\{I_d\}$, respectively.
\end{definition}

The notation $A_J$ denotes a set for an extended node index, whereas $A_I$ denotes a single matrix for a positive word.
A word is \emph{encoded} by $J$ precisely when it belongs to $\mathcal{W}(J)$.
Its expanded length is
\[
    |I|=\sum_{j_s>0}1+\sum_{j_s<0}k_{-j_s}e_s.
\]
Thus node depth $l$, generator exponents $e_s$, and product length $|I|$ are different quantities.
Equality of two matrices alone does not identify their words or their lengths.

\begin{definition}
    \label{def:multiIndexExtension}
    For words $J$ and $K$, the expression $[J,K]$ denotes their concatenation, with nested brackets flattened.
    A child of node $J$ has index $[J,j]$ for one $j\in\mathcal{J}$.
    \label{def:childType}
    The child is \emph{positive} when $j>0$ and a \emph{generator child} when $j<0$.
\end{definition}

For example, if $n=2$ and $m=1$, the node $[-1,2,-1,1]$ encodes
\[
    \{A_1G_1^sA_2G_1^r:r,s\in\N_0\}.
\]
Its depth is four, while its words have length $2+k_1(r+s)$.
The number of blocks is fixed even though the generator exponents are unbounded.

\subsection{Finite Certificate Trees and Leafage}

\begin{definition}[Finite $\AG$-tree]
    \label{def:agTree}
    Relative to the fixed generator words and bounds above, an $\AG$-tree $T$ is a finite rooted tree of extended index words with the following properties:
    \begin{itemize}
        \item The root is $[]$ and represents $\{I_d\}$.
        It is internal.
        \item Every internal node $J$ has either all $n$ positive children $[J,1],\dots,[J,n]$, or a nonempty selection of generator children $[J,-i]$.
        \item A generator child is never a leaf.
    \end{itemize}
\end{definition}

The full tree $T_{\mathcal{A}}$ is infinite, but every $\AG$-tree used as a certificate here is finite.
Requiring an internal root excludes the vacuous tree $\{I_d\}$, whose norm bound would give no information about $\mathcal{A}$.

\begin{definition}[Coverage and leafage]
    \label{def:coveredNode}
    A node $J$ is \emph{covered} if it has a proper ancestor $Q$ such that
    \[
        \mathcal{W}(J)\subseteq\mathcal{W}(Q).
    \]
    Otherwise it is \emph{uncovered}.
    The \emph{leafage} $\mathcal{L}(T)$ is the collection of matrix sets $A_J$ represented by uncovered leaves $J$.

    This word-preserving condition retains each expanded word and its length when it is represented at the ancestor.
    It is stronger than the matrix-set inclusion $A_J\subseteq A_Q$ used in \citet{MoellerReif2014TreeBasedJSR,MejstrikReif2025HybridApproachJSR}.
    Equality of matrices arising from different words alone does not establish coverage.
    A typical covered node completes one more repetition of a generator word: $[Q,-i,H_i]$ is covered by $[Q,-i]$.
    The expanded words are identical, with the generator exponent increased by one.
\end{definition}

For a matrix set $L$ and a vector $v$, define $\lVert L\rVert=\sup_{P\in L}\lVert P\rVert$ and $\lVert Lv\rVert=\sup_{P\in L}\lVert Pv\rVert$, using a given vector norm and its induced matrix norm.
The supremum may be infinite.
A leaf bound therefore concerns every represented generator power.
For an empty leafage, the maximum of the leaf bounds is defined as $0$.

\begin{definition}
    \label{def:oneBoundedTree}
    An $\AG$-tree is \emph{$1$-bounded} in a chosen submultiplicative matrix norm if $\lVert L\rVert\leq1$ for every $L\in\mathcal{L}(T)$.
    It is \emph{strictly $1$-bounded} if $\max_{L\in\mathcal{L}(T)}\lVert L\rVert<1$.
\end{definition}

\begin{example}[A finite generator tree]
    \label{ex:finiteJordanTree}
    Consider $\mathcal{A}=\{A_1,A_2\}=\{A,B\}$ with
    \[
        A=\begin{bmatrix}1&1\\0&1\end{bmatrix},\qquad
        B=\begin{bmatrix}0&0\\0&1/2\end{bmatrix},\qquad G_1=A.
    \]
    The generator word is $H_1=[1]$ and $\rho(G_1)=1$.
    In the induced maximum norm,
    \[
        A^r=\begin{bmatrix}1&r\\0&1\end{bmatrix},\qquad
        \lVert A^r\rVert_\infty=r+1,\qquad BA^r=B.
    \]
    The following four-node tree has a generator child and two positive leaves.
    \begin{center}
        \begin{forest}
for tree={grow'=0, child anchor=west, parent anchor=east,
l sep=14pt, s sep=10pt, edge={->, >=latex}, font=\small}
            [{$\{I_2\}$}
                [{$\{A^r:r\geq0\}$}, edge label={node[midway,above]{$-1$}}
                    [{$\{A^{r+1}:r\geq0\}$ covered}, edge label={node[midway,above]{$1$}}]
                    [{$\{BA^r:r\geq0\}=\{B\}$}, edge label={node[midway,below]{$2$}}]
                ]
            ]
        \end{forest}
    \end{center}
    The words at $[-1,1]$ are nonempty strings of $1$'s, all already represented by $[-1]$.
    The other leaf $[-1,2]$ has norm $1/2$, so the tree is strictly $1$-bounded.
    Its internal generator family has unbounded norm but admits the linear bound $r+1$.
    In particular, spectral radius at most $1$ does not imply uniformly bounded powers.
\end{example}

\section{Algorithmic Formulation}
\label{sec:finiteAlgorithmStructure}

The search starts from the root and its positive children.
A positive node can close when it is covered or its entire matrix family has norm at most $1$.
All other nodes are expanded.
Generator children must be expanded even if they already satisfy a bound.
The child-selection rule chooses either all positive children or a nonempty selection of generator children.

\begin{algorithm}[ht]
    \caption{Finite-tree certificate search}
    \label{alg:finiteTree}
    \begin{algorithmic}
        \State \Input{$\mathcal{A}$, admissible generators $\mathcal{G}$, and a matrix norm}
        \State $T\gets\{[],[1],\dots,[n]\}$
        \State $Q\gets\{[1],\dots,[n]\}$
        \While{$Q\neq\emptyset$}
            \State Remove a node $J$ from $Q$
            \If{$J$ is a generator child}
                \State $\mathcal{C}\gets\textproc{selectChildren}(J)$
            \ElsIf{$J$ is covered or $\lVert A_J\rVert\leq1$}
                \State Close $J$ as a leaf
                \State Continue with the next node
            \Else
                \State $\mathcal{C}\gets\textproc{selectChildren}(J)$
            \EndIf
            \State $\mathcal{D}\gets\{[J,j]:j\in\mathcal{C}\}$
            \State $T\xleftarrow{\cup}\mathcal{D}$
            \State $Q\xleftarrow{\cup}\mathcal{D}$
        \EndWhile
        \State \Return $T$
    \end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

This is a mathematical search procedure with exact coverage and norm tests.
Testing a generator family requires control of all its powers, not only a finite sample.
If the procedure returns, its finite tree satisfies Definition~\ref{def:agTree} and is $1$-bounded.
The search may also continue indefinitely.

\section{Termination Theory}

\subsection{Bounds for Encoded Products}

The first lemma uses only finite depth and the generator spectral bounds.
It does not assume $1$-bounded leafage or any spectral bound on the base matrices.
The bound on generator powers follows from Jordan normal form \citep{HornJohnson1985MatrixAnalysis}.

\begin{lemma}[Common polynomial bound]
    \label{lem:finitePolynomialBound}
    Fix a finite $\AG$-tree $T$ and a submultiplicative matrix norm.
    There is a positive, monotonically nondecreasing polynomial $p_T$ such that
    \[
        \lVert A_I\rVert\leq p_T(k)
    \]
    for every word $I\in\mathcal{I}^k$ encoded by any node of $T$, including $k=0$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
    Bound the positive factors by the common constant
    \[
        C_{\mathcal{A}}=\max\{1,\lVert A_1\rVert,\dots,\lVert A_n\rVert\}.
    \]
    Since $\rho(G_i)\leq1$, Jordan normal form bounds each generator power by a polynomial of degree at most $d-1$.
    There are finitely many generators, so one constant $C_{\mathcal{G}}\geq1$ satisfies
    \[
        \lVert G_i^r\rVert\leq C_{\mathcal{G}}(1+r)^{d-1}
        \quad\text{for }1\leq i\leq m,\ r\in\N_0.
    \]
    Set $C=\max\{C_{\mathcal{A}},C_{\mathcal{G}},\lVert I_d\rVert\}$ and let $h\geq1$ be the maximum node depth of $T$.
    Define
    \[
        q(x)=C(1+x)^{d-1},\qquad p_T(x)=q(x)^h.
    \]
    Take an encoded word $I\in\mathcal{W}(J)$ of expanded length $k$ at a nonroot node $J=[j_1,\dots,j_l]$.
    Each positive position occurs once and has norm at most $C_{\mathcal{A}}\leq q(k)$.
    At a negative position, the expansion contributes $k_{-j_s}e_s$ letters, so $e_s\leq k$ and its matrix norm is at most $q(e_s)\leq q(k)$.
    Submultiplicativity and $l\leq h$ therefore give
    \[
        \lVert A_I\rVert\leq q(k)^l\leq q(k)^h=p_T(k).
    \]
    The second inequality uses $q(k)\geq1$.
    For the root, $\lVert I_d\rVert\leq C\leq p_T(0)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
    The lemma allows base matrices of spectral radius greater than $1$.
    A $1$-bounded certificate cannot exist for such a family, since its upper JSR bound would contradict the lower inequality in \eqref{eq:three_member}.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}
    Finite depth is essential for this argument.
    For instance, the infinite positive chain generated by $A=[2]$ contains $A^k$ of norm $2^k$.
    A truncation at depth $h$ has the constant bound $2^h$.
    Individual spectral bounds on base matrices do not permit arbitrary infinite trees either: the switching example in Chapter~\ref{ch:introduction} already has exponentially growing products.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}
    If all generator spectral radii are strictly below $1$, their powers are uniformly bounded, and the fixed finite tree even admits a constant bound.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Prefix--Leaf Decomposition}

The next lemma identifies an uncovered leaf factor whenever a word is not encoded by any node.
In index order, a prefix is read first and corresponds to a rightmost factor of the matrix product.

\begin{lemma}[One-step decomposition]
    \label{lem:finitePrefixLeaf}
    Let $T$ be a finite $\AG$-tree.
    Every nonempty positive word $I$ is either encoded by a node of $T$, or has a decomposition $I=[U,J]$ such that $U$ is a nonempty word encoded by an uncovered leaf and $|J|<|I|$.
    Thus $A_I=A_JA_U$ in the second case.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
    Every one-letter word is encoded somewhere in $T$.
    Indeed, start at the internal root and follow generator children with exponent zero until reaching a node with positive children.
    Such a node must occur because the tree is finite and generator children are not leaves.
    Its full set of positive children encodes every one-letter word.

    Suppose $I$ is not encoded by any node.
    Choose its longest encoded prefix $U$, and among nodes encoding $U$ choose a node $K$ of greatest depth.
    The preceding observation gives $1\leq|U|<|I|$.
    The node $K$ has no generator child, since exponent zero would encode $U$ at a deeper node.
    It has no positive children either, since the next letter of $I$ would extend $U$ to a longer encoded prefix.
    Hence $K$ is a leaf.

    If $K$ were covered, let $Q$ be an ancestor with $\mathcal{W}(K)\subseteq\mathcal{W}(Q)$.
    On the path from $Q$ to $K$, consider the parent $R$ of the first positive child.
    Such a child exists because the leaf $K$ is positive.
    All earlier edges on this path are generator edges, so exponent zero gives $\mathcal{W}(Q)\subseteq\mathcal{W}(R)$.
    Consequently $R$ encodes $U$ and has all positive children.
    Appending the next letter of $I$ again contradicts maximality of $U$.
    Thus $K$ is uncovered, and the complementary word $J$ is strictly shorter than $I$.
\end{proof}

Repeating the lemma on the remaining word yields
\[
    I=[I_1,\dots,I_s,R],\qquad
    A_I=A_RA_{I_s}\cdots A_{I_1},
\]
where each $I_j$ is a nonempty uncovered-leaf word and $R$ is encoded by a node of $T$.
The number of removed factors is at most $|I|$.
It may be zero when the entire word is already encoded by $T$.
The remaining factor $A_R$ lies on the left and may come from an internal node.

\subsection{Upper-Bound Certificate}

\begin{theorem}[Finite-tree certificate]
    \label{thm:finiteTreeTermination}
    If a finite $\AG$-tree is $1$-bounded in a submultiplicative matrix norm, then $\JSR(\mathcal{A})\leq1$.
    If some product has averaged spectral radius $1$, then $\JSR(\mathcal{A})=1$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
    For a word $I$ of length $k\geq1$, use the decomposition above and the common polynomial from Lemma~\ref{lem:finitePolynomialBound}.
    The leaf factors have norm at most $1$, and $|R|\leq k$.
    Hence
    \[
        \lVert A_I\rVert
        \leq\lVert A_R\rVert\prod_{j=1}^s\lVert A_{I_j}\rVert
        \leq p_T(|R|)\leq p_T(k).
    \]
    This holds for every word of length $k$, so
    \[
        \JSR(\mathcal{A})
        \leq\lim_{k\to\infty}p_T(k)^{1/k}=1.
    \]
    A product with averaged spectral radius $1$ supplies the reverse inequality by \eqref{eq:three_member}.
\end{proof}

This is the finite-tree implication of \citet{MoellerReif2014TreeBasedJSR}.
It verifies a tree found by the search, but a child-selection heuristic may run indefinitely.

\begin{example}[Leaf factors and an internal remainder]
    \label{ex:finiteLeafDecomposition}
    Continue Example~\ref{ex:finiteJordanTree} and consider the word with matrix product
    \[
        P=A^3BA^2BA^4
        =\underbrace{A^3}_{R}
        \underbrace{(BA^2)}_{L_2}
        \underbrace{(BA^4)}_{L_1}.
    \]
    The factors $L_1,L_2$ are leaf factors, and $R$ is the remainder.
    Read the product from the right: the first run of $A$'s stays in the generator family, and the next $B$ completes a leaf factor.
    Repeat after removing that factor.
    The last run $A^3$ remains at an internal node.
    Its norm is $4$, while both leaf factors have norm $1/2$, giving $\lVert P\rVert_\infty\leq4(1/2)^2=1$.
    For an arbitrary word of length $k$, the same argument leaves $A^q$ with $q\leq k$, and thus gives $\lVert P\rVert_\infty\leq k+1$.
    The all-$A$ word has norm exactly $k+1$, so a constant bound would fail.
    Nevertheless $(k+1)^{1/k}\to1$, and $\rho(A)=1$ supplies the lower bound: $\JSR(\{A,B\})=1$.
\end{example}

\section{Search and Implementation}

\begin{remark}
    \label{rem:moller}
    The procedure \textproc{selectChildren} must respect the branching rule and the fixed admissible generator set.
    A concrete search strategy is described in \citet[Sections~4.1--4.2, Algorithms~4.3--4.4]{Moeller2015NewStrategyExactJSR}.
    M\"oller uses a local decision routine to choose a generator child or positive children, without storing an entire search tree in advance.
    Covered or bounded nodes close their branches, and all positive siblings must be processed before returning to the parent.
    If a depth limit is reached without closure, backtracking revises the choice at the nearest ancestor with a generator child and retains the other closed branches.

    Her practical routine controls the earliest generator level and the number of generator positions along a path.
    It can first follow several positive repetitions of a generator word before introducing the corresponding generator child.
    The resulting family contains only the tail of the generator powers, which can be easier to bound than all powers starting at exponent $0$.
    For example, if the powers converge and the limiting product has norm strictly below $1$, a sufficiently late tail satisfies the bound.
    These choices affect search cost and whether a certificate is found within the chosen limits.
    The schematic procedure here does not prescribe this backtracking strategy or guarantee a complete search.
    Theorem~\ref{thm:finiteTreeTermination} concerns only the returned certificate.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}
    \label{rem:finiteTreeDecomposition}
    The decomposition need not be unique.
    The proof requires only an actual word partition with nonempty leaf words and a node-encoded remainder.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}
    \label{rem:finiteGeneratorNecessity}
    A finite tree may close even when no invariant polytope exists at the candidate scaling \citep{MoellerReif2014TreeBasedJSR,MejstrikReif2025HybridApproachJSR}.
    In Example~\ref{ex:finiteJordanTree}, powers of $A$ are unbounded.
    Invariance of a bounded norm unit ball would instead bound every power.
    The tree certificate succeeds because only its uncovered leafage must satisfy the norm bound.
\end{remark}
